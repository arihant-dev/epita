Mini-case study: 
You are the manager of an Indian team in charge of the development of IT projects.  You are based in France; part of your team is located in the same building as you; the rest is at a distance. You collaborate on a daily basis with French colleagues in other departments (Finance, Marketing, Sales…) and with French clients. Tensions are frequent between the Indians and the French due to misunderstandings, different work styles, communication styles, and distance. There are also English language and accents issues. All these elements directly impact work relationships, trust, motivation, and the lead time of projects.
The French are strict on deadlines although they are often 5-10 minutes late to meetings and prefer a structured approach. They debate at length during meetings on the best approach and as a consequence, decisions are protracted. They tend to have a quite direct communication style and to express strong opinions during meetings. They also demand regularly written reports. The Indians tend to say ‘yes’ to everything but prevent from committing clearly, sometimes retaining information on technical difficulties they are encountering so as to maintain harmony. They are also display a flexible time orientation and tend to be late with deadlines (or deliver on time but with a level of quality and structure considered as insufficient by the French). They also tend to be late by up to 30 minutes to meetings if hierarchy is not present. Finally, they sometimes keep quiet during meetings, sometimes they tend to speak for a very long time in an indirect way.

 
What culturally drives the behaviors of each side? What cultural challenges contribute to misunderstandings and tensions? 
What could you suggest putting in place as team leader so that both parties collaborate more efficiently with one another? 
What actions could help rebuild trust?
