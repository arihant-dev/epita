## 2. The Case Study

**File:** [`Cross Border Management - Final Paper - Mini Case Study.md`](./Cross%20Border%20Management%20-%20Final%20Paper%20-%20Mini%20Case%20Study.md)

**Scenario:** You are the manager of an Indian IT team based in France. Part of the team is co-located, part is remote. You collaborate daily with French colleagues (Finance, Marketing, Sales) and French clients. Tensions exist due to:
- Misunderstandings and different communication styles
- Different work styles and time orientations
- Distance (remote team members)
- English language and accent issues

**Specific behaviours described in the case:**

| French behaviours | Indian behaviours |
|---|---|
| Strict on deadlines | Flexible with deadlines, deliver late or with insufficient quality |
| 5–10 min late to meetings (tolerated) | Up to 30 min late if hierarchy not present |
| Prefer structured approach | Say "yes" without full commitment |
| Debate at length, protracted decisions | Retain info on technical difficulties to maintain harmony |
| Direct communication, strong opinions | Quiet in meetings OR speak at length indirectly |
| Demand written reports | — |

**Three questions the paper must answer:**
1. What culturally drives the behaviours of each side? What cultural challenges contribute to misunderstandings and tensions?
2. What could you suggest putting in place as team leader so that both parties collaborate more efficiently?
3. What actions could help rebuild trust?

## 5. Decisions Made (and Why)

These decisions were made during the first iteration. Future agents should understand them before changing anything.

| Decision | Reasoning |
|---|---|
| **Breadth over depth** in Part 1 | The case study touches all 7 dimensions explicitly. Covering all of them briefly demonstrates analytical completeness. The professor rewards "thoroughness." Depth is concentrated in Part 2 (recommendations). |
| **Izzat / honor dropped entirely** | Arihant used it in homework context but said it's "not a good factor to analyze anything on." It's not a GlobeSmart or COF dimension. We do not mention it anywhere in the paper. |
| **Unified team paper** | v1 is written as a single team submission. Teammates can later review and develop sections based on their own COF profiles if they want. |
| **Paper only (no oral defense yet)** | Arihant asked to focus on the paper first. Oral defense prep is deferred. |
| **COF profile used because Arihant = the manager** | The case study puts you in the manager role. Arihant's COF shows his cultural positioning relative to both the French and Indian teams, which informs how he'd need to style-switch. |
| **Real experiences over hypotheticals** | The professor penalizes generic/vague recommendations. We used Arihant's actual Thriwe experiences (product scope failure, silent engineering manager, sprint rituals) to ground every recommendation. |
| **No AI fluff** | Strict instruction from Arihant: "NO AI FLUFF, NO AI REPETITION, NO AI ADJECTIVES, NO AI SLOP." The paper is written in direct, plain prose. No "foster mutual understanding," "embrace diversity," "leverage synergies," or any similar padding. |
| **MIS factor included as section 1.7** | Not a GlobeSmart dimension but taught in Class #4 as the mechanism through which individual mismatches compound. It ties Part 1 together and sets up the urgency for Part 2. |
| **Trompenaars 4R referenced but not the organizing structure** | Referenced in section 2.1 as the principle behind creating ground rules (Recognize phase). Not used as the paper's structure because the recommendations are organized by practical problem area, which is more useful. |

## 7. Arihant's Real Experiences (Used in Paper)

These are the concrete professional experiences that ground the recommendations. They come from Arihant's 5 years as a software engineer at Thriwe (India, 2019–2024) and his current Masters at EPITA (France).

### Experience 1: Product Scope Failure (used in §2.2)
A developer said "yes" to sales/product team without understanding the full feature list. Client wanted a secure communication channel between companies. Team delivered a social network where people could see each other's companies but couldn't communicate. Client was "underimpressed and borderline angry." **Fix:** Multiple context meetings before scope definition, user flow journey mapping, sprints with stakeholder demos at sprint close.

### Experience 2: Silent Engineering Manager (used in §2.3)
On a call with a European AWS team, Arihant's engineering manager had too many tasks that week and sat through the entire meeting without contributing. Arihant couldn't overextend himself (junior in an Indian startup). **Fix:** Started creating context docs, implementation docs, and question docs to carry into meetings, assigning discussion points to specific people.

### Experience 3: Directness Gap at EPITA (context for §2.1, §2.4)
Arihant has always been more direct than typical Indian professionals (startup background). At EPITA, he observes Indian students being indirect with French professors, which frustrates both the professors and himself. Students come across as "too casual about serious tasks and deadlines."

### Experience 4: Sprint Rituals (used in §2.6)
Regular standups, contextual docs, guidelines, sprint check-ins, and sprint spillover meetings worked well at Thriwe. Core insight: "everyone makes mistakes but if we don't learn from them and improve on them we are still stuck in the same vicious cycle of degradation and would not be able to deliver 100%."

## 10. Key Course Frameworks Reference

For anyone iterating who hasn't read all the materials:

### GlobeSmart 5 Dimensions
1. **Independent ↔ Interdependent** — How do I derive my identity? (me vs. we)
2. **Egalitarian ↔ Status** — How should power be distributed?
3. **Risk ↔ Certainty** — How do I handle ambiguity?
4. **Direct ↔ Indirect** — How do I handle disagreement?
5. **Task ↔ Relationship** — Do I address work or connection first?

### COF ↔ GlobeSmart Conversion (3 reversed dimensions)
| GlobeSmart | COF (reversed) |
|---|---|
| Egalitarian → Status | Hierarchy → Equality |
| Risk → Certainty | Stability → Change |
| Task → Relationship | Being → Doing |

### Trompenaars' 4R Method
1. **Recognize** — See that differences exist
2. **Respect** — Understand the reasons behind different behaviours
3. **Reconcile** — Find solutions that work for both sides
4. **Realize** — Put the reconciliation into practice

### MIS Factor (Vicious Cycle)
MISperception → MISinterpretation → MISevaluation → MIStrust → cycle repeats

### France's GlobeSmart Profile (from Class #4 & #5)
- Moderate interdependent
- Leans toward status
- Moderate risk tolerance
- **Direct for criticism, indirect otherwise**
- Moderately task-oriented (but lunch is sacred)

### French Core Values (from Class #5)
- Hierarchy (earned through intellectual credibility)
- Thinking / intellectual approach (l'esprit critique, Cartesian reasoning)
- Style and sophistication
- The art of debate
- Individual creativity
- Pride in culture and arts
- The art of living (joie de vivre)
