# 1. Personal Examples

## Direct vs Indirect
At my company in India, my manager would hint at problems during team meetings. At EPITA, professors tell you directly what you did wrong, right in front of the class.

**India = Indirect, France = Direct**

## Task vs Relationship
Client meetings in India started with 15 minutes of personal chat. French startups skip that and jump into the agenda.

**India = Relationship-focused, France = Task-focused**

## Independent vs Interdependent
In a French team, I was expected to make decisions on my own and be accountable for them. At Thriwe in India, every decision had to align with the team's consensus, even for individual tasks.

**France = Independent, India = Interdependent**

# 2. Cultural Dimensions

**Direct vs Indirect**
- Indirect: avoids disagreement; keeps harmony
- Direct: states disagreement openly

**Risk vs Certainty**
- Risk-oriented: comfortable with ambiguity, figures things out as they go
- Certainty-oriented: wants clear processes and approvals before acting

**Task vs Relationship**
- Task-oriented: focuses on outcomes, separates personal feelings from work
- Relationship-oriented: needs personal connection before working together

**Independent vs Interdependent**
- Independent: individual choice and identity matter most
- Interdependent: identity comes from the group, decisions shaped by group membership

# 3. Honor / Social Dignity

Drives indirect communication, hierarchy respect, and relationship-building through favors. Feedback is private to protect reputation; seniority is acknowledged.

# 4. Context Levels and Communication

**High-context:** Meaning comes from shared background, tone, and what's unsaid.
**Low-context:** Everything must be explicit in the words.

Note: Different from directness—context is shared background knowledge; directness is how disagreement is handled.
