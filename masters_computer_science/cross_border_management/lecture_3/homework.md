**India = Relationship-focused, France = Task-focused**

# 3. Core Value of Country of Origin — India: Honor / Social Dignity (Izzat)

In Indian professional culture, reputation and face matter enormously. Feedback is almost always given in private to avoid embarrassing someone publicly. Seniority is acknowledged in how you address people (sir/ma'am even for peers who are slightly senior). Favors build social credit. This drives indirectness — saying something blunt publicly can damage someone's standing, which is seen as worse than leaving a problem unsaid.

Example: At Thriwe, when a teammate made a mistake that caused a production bug, the manager pulled him aside privately. The post-mortem meeting never mentioned names — it focused on "the process gap." In France, at EPITA, a professor called out a student's incorrect architecture diagram by name in front of everyone, without hesitation.

# 4. High vs Low Context — Key Difference from Direct/Indirect

**High-context:** Meaning is carried by the whole situation — tone, silence, relationship history, who's in the room, what's not said. Words are only part of the message.

**Low-context:** Words carry everything. Messages are explicit, precise, and meant to be taken at face value. Repetition is fine if it helps clarity.

**How this differs from Direct/Indirect:**
- Direct/Indirect = how you handle disagreement or negative information
- High/Low context = how much of the meaning lives outside the words

You can be indirect AND low-context (e.g. diplomatic but explicit). You can be high-context AND relatively direct (e.g. Japan is high-context but uses very clear signals within that context). They're separate axes.
