## Concept - Dominant Design

- Example - Dominant Design for music players shifted from Audio Cassette CDs to MP3 players to streaming services.

## Claude Shannon's Information Theory

- It has been formalised by Claude E. Shannon in a seminal 1948 paper "A Mathematical Theory of Communication".
- Additional developments to the information theory include those of Adrian Mc Donough(Information economics, 1963) who defines information as the meeting between data and a problem. In such a setting, information has value only if it helps to reduce uncertainty in decision making.
- Information theory quantifies information and its transmission.
- Key concepts include entropy (measure of uncertainty), redundancy (extra bits for error correction), and channel capacity (maximum data rate).
- Applications include data compression, error detection/correction, and cryptography.

## Implications of Shannon's Information Theory

1. Relationship between data, information, and knowledge:
   - Data: Raw facts and figures without context.
   - Information: Processed data that is meaningful.
   - Knowledge: Information that is understood and can be applied.
   - Signals => Bits of Data => Information => Knowledge
2. It became possible to reconstruct original messages from noisy transmissions using error-correcting codes.
3. It laid the groundwork for modern digital communication systems, including the internet and mobile networks.

## Moore's Law

- Gordon Moore, co-founder of Intel, observed in 1965 that the number of transistors on a microchip doubles approximately every two years, while the cost of computers is halved.
- This trend has driven exponential growth in computing power, enabling advancements in various fields such as artificial intelligence, data analytics, and mobile technology.
- While physical and economic limits may eventually slow this trend, innovations in chip design and new materials continue to push the boundaries of computing performance.

## Metcalfe's Law Reed's Law

- Metcalfe: The value of a telecommunications network is proportional to the square of the number of connected users of the system (n^2).
- Reed: The utility of large networks, particularly social networks, can scale exponentially with the size of the network.

## Implications of Metcalfe's and Reed's Laws

1. The importance of network effects in technology adoption:
   - As more users join a network, the value of the network increases for all users, leading to rapid adoption and growth.
2. Strategies for building and scaling networks:
   - Focus on user acquisition and retention to maximize network size.
   - Encourage user interaction and content creation to enhance network value.
3. The role of social networks and online platforms in modern communication:
   - Platforms like Facebook, Twitter, and LinkedIn leverage network effects to connect users and facilitate information sharing on a global scale.

## General Purpose Technologies (GPTs)

- GPTs are technologies that have broad applications and can drive significant economic and social change.
- Examples include the steam engine, electricity, and the internet.
- Characteristics of GPTs:
  - Pervasiveness: Used across a wide range of industries.
  - Improvement: Continually enhanced over time. (Inherent potential for technical improvements and innovations)
  - Innovation complementaries: Enable the creation of new products and services.
- Impact of GPTs on economic growth and societal transformation:
  - GPTs can lead to increased productivity, job creation, and shifts in labor markets.
  - They often require complementary innovations and infrastructure to fully realize their potential.
- GPT's require innovations to happen at all levels of the value chain, therefore they deliver their benefits with a delay.
